## Claim 1 — the comparison is scoped to two named production systems on one target, by the publisher itself

The post names its two subjects and stops there: "For this blog post, we will limit ourselves to two concrete systems: Fuzzilli, a JavaScript engine fuzzer, and BigSleep, an agentic bug finding system" (S093.txt:22), and "We also limit ourselves to V8 as a target" (S093.txt:23). Fuzzilli is described as a generative JavaScript-engine fuzzer and BigSleep as an "LLM-based bug finding system" (S093.txt:16); both run as production pipelines on newly added code (S093.txt:22), with Fuzzilli spanning many instances and BigSleep running once per day (S093.txt:22). The post is a comparison of these two systems on one engine, not a benchmark of fuzzing against agentic analysis in general.

Falsifiable rule: a Project Zero publication that compares these two systems under a different date, byline or target, or a post that does not limit itself to Fuzzilli, BigSleep and V8, contradicts the capture as served on the recorded fetch date. Rejected over-generalization: the entry does not read the post as a general fuzzer-versus-agent benchmark — the post's own scope sentence (S093.txt:22) and target sentence (S093.txt:23) exclude that reading.

## Claim 2 — the six-month production window: raw reports favour BigSleep, type-filtered reports favour Fuzzilli, and actionable Type-Vuln reports are roughly a tie at 45 versus 37 with a ~25% overlap

The production data is drawn from two Chromium hotlist queries over "the six months from April to September 2026" (S093.txt:26), each carrying the same date range in its query: Fuzzilli `hotlistid:5433121 created:2026-04-01..2026-09-30` and BigSleep `hotlistid:8067258 created:2026-04-01..2026-09-30` (S093.txt:27, :28). The post prints the three successive views: raw reports 316 for Fuzzilli and 441 for BigSleep (S093.txt:30, :31); reports restricted to `type:vulnerability` 178 and 95 (S093.txt:33, :34); and, after excluding duplicates, WAI reports and reports without a fix commit, the actionable Type-Vuln picture "roughly a tie (small advantage BigSleep) at the moment: 45 vs. 37" (S093.txt:40, Q3). The summary states the same pair as "BigSleep found more vulnerabilities than Fuzzilli (45 versus 37) with a 25% overlap in the specific bugs reported" (S093.txt:16, Q2), and the overlap section gives the production measure as "roughly 25% (20 out of a total of 81)" (S093.txt:51, Q4).

Falsifiable rule: re-running the two printed hotlist queries over the same window and applying the post's own steps (drop duplicates, WAI and fix-less reports; keep Type-Vuln) yields different actionable counts or a different overlap fraction, or a reader citing 441 or 316 as vulnerability counts contradicts the post's own narrowing at S093.txt:33-34 and S093.txt:40. Rejected over-generalization: the entry does not treat these numbers as an efficiency measure — the post states that "it’s challenging to make a comparison at this stage in the research" on cost-per-bug (S093.txt:41), so no cost or effort claim is admitted; and the raw report counts are not vulnerability counts.

## Claim 3 — the re-discovery experiment: 112 historic Fuzzilli bugs, 25 attempts per commit, ~62% re-discovered plus ~12% different bugs, with a bounded memorization check

The experiment narrows a historic sample in three printed steps: around 1150 raw Fuzzilli bugs from early 2023 to mid-2026 (S093.txt:55), "roughly 600 vulnerabilities" after the `type:vulnerability` restriction (S093.txt:56), "132 historic bugs" after the reproduce-in-the-agent's-environment filter (S093.txt:60), and "a final number of 112 bugs" after deduplication (S093.txt:61, Q5). The run is bounded at "25 attempts on each commit" (S093.txt:65, Q6) using "the same underlying model: Gemini 3.6 flash" (S093.txt:65), whose knowledge cutoff is March 2026 (S093.txt:71, Q8). The result is stated as "BigSleep was able to re-discover ~62% of the original bugs, while finding a different (but by now fixed) bug for another ~12% of commits. On around 26% of the commits it failed to find any bugs" (S093.txt:68, Q7). A memorization check over time spans finds "no statistically significant amount of memorization pre-2026" for that model (S093.txt:71). Prompt-variant follow-ups at 5 attempts per bug add "10 additional bugs in total: 8 from the different starting points and 7 with the rephrased prompt" (S093.txt:82, Q10), moving the share of "correct" bugs found to ~70% and any-bug discovery to ~84% of starting points, with the post's own caution that equivalent fuzzer tweaks could also raise discovery (S093.txt:84). The experiment summary states overlap "around 60% of historic Fuzzilli bugs (70% with additional runs that used different prompts)" (S093.txt:88, Q11).

Falsifiable rule: the printed sample sizes (1150, ~600, 132, 112), the attempt budget (25), the re-discovery shares (~62%, ~12%, ~26%) and the follow-up counts (10 = 8 + 7 with 5 in both) must appear in the capture; a reader who reports a different attempt budget, sample size or share, or who reports the experiment as bidirectional, contradicts S093.txt:53-68. Rejected over-generalization: the ~62%/~70% figures are a re-discovery rate over a filtered historic Fuzzilli sample, not a general agent recall rate; the post itself says the follow-up results "should be treated with some caution" (S093.txt:84). The reverse direction (re-discovering BigSleep bugs with Fuzzilli) was not run: the post says it "is more difficult to set up, since fuzzers cannot easily be given a similar 'starting point'" (S093.txt:53), so no symmetric-overlap claim is admitted.

## Claim 4 — the post's operating recommendation: fuzzing and LLM agents remain complementary, and vendors should run both

The post's stated conclusion is that the two approaches complement each other: "fuzzing and LLMs are still complementary approaches" (S093.txt:16, Q12); "We recommend vendors perform both fuzzing and agentic analysis of their code to maximize the number of vulnerabilities that get reported before they impact users" (S093.txt:16, Q13); and the conclusion repeats that "the two approaches remain complementary, and specialized fuzzing continues to provide value by finding bugs quickly" (S093.txt:281, Q14). The overlap is stated as uneven rather than uniform: "Bugs that are difficult for a fuzzer to find are more likely to also be difficult to find for an agent, so there’s more overlap on 'easy' bugs" (S093.txt:90), and "The overlap seems to be smaller in the JIT Compilers and higher in the rest of the engine" (S093.txt:91); the related difficulty result is that "Bugs that are hard to find for a fuzzer are likely also hard to find for an AI agent" (S093.txt:74, Q9). The post expects the agent side to keep moving: "we expect further model improvements, potentially coupled with better prompting and more flexible harnesses" (S093.txt:281, Q15).

Falsifiable rule: a claim that the post recommends replacing fuzzing with agents, or that it establishes agent superiority over fuzzing, contradicts the recommendation sentence (S093.txt:16, Q13) and the conclusion sentence (S093.txt:281, Q14), both of which say the approaches remain complementary and recommend both. Rejected over-generalization: the post is not vendor policy, a bounty-program rule, an intake channel or a severity ladder, and it states no intake or reward guidance — it is a research comparison with an operating recommendation for vendors.

## Limits
