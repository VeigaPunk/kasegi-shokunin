## Evidence and falsifiable boundary

- Frozen C2 requires F07 to use the *current* CNA rules and F08 to use official CRA text; it states absent official text is unprovable, not replaceable with secondary authority (`.ufo/waves/round-23/claims.json:5`). The round task prompts also explicitly say CRA manufacturer duty is not researcher authorization and prohibit external contact/testing.
- The official CNA snapshot is `corpus/source-snapshots/round-23/S030.txt`: its header identifies `CNA_Rules_v4.2.0.pdf`, Version 4.2.0, approved 2026-08-20, effective 2026-08-25 (lines 1–16). Section 3.1 defines CNA scope as which vulnerabilities a CNA assigns CVE IDs/publishes CVE records for (lines 61–66); 3.2.6 says “The CVE Program itself does not follow or require a specific Vulnerability disclosure policy” (lines 120–132). This is assignment/governance scope, not a grant to test products or assets.
- The current CNA entry preserves that boundary: its limits say the recheck proves captured-source support only, not current online freshness, CVE assignment, bounty eligibility, or permission to test (`corpus/knowledge/cna-assignment-and-vulnerability-rules.md:58–61`). It says CNA assignment rules are not universal bounty-eligibility criteria and that external requests remain operator-owned (`:8–17`, `:33–46`). Those are appropriately non-actionable.
- The official CRA snapshot, EUR-Lex Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, Article 14, places the active-exploitation and severe-incident reporting duties on “A manufacturer” (snapshot `corpus/source-snapshots/round-23/S029.txt`, Article 14(1)–(4); local view lines 2–24). Article 15 separately permits voluntary *notification* by other natural/legal persons (Article 15(1)–(2)); Article 15(5) expressly preserves criminal-offence prevention/investigation/detection/prosecution; Article 17(4) only says the mere act of notification does not subject the notifying person to increased liability. These are reporting rules, not authorization for research/testing or blanket immunity for conduct. Article 71 sets Article 14 application at 2026-09-11 and general CRA application at 2027-12-11 (snapshot lines 120–126).
- The CRA entry correctly rejects “CRA requires every researcher to publish a vulnerability within 24 hours, and therefore authorizes testing,” states Article 15 is not an asset-scope/testing grant, makes no legal-protection conclusion, and forbids target contact, scanning, login, submission, or account operation (`corpus/knowledge/cra-manufacturer-reporting-duties.md:62–76`). Its Article 16 recipient/platform passage also explicitly says it is not a direction to submit (`:36–39`).
- The controlling corpus criterion is Q6: documentation-only; never instruct probing, scanning, logging in, or submitting against any target (`corpus/QUALITY.md:19`). Apply Q4 attribution as well (`:17`): neither CNA rules nor CRA statutory duties should be generalized into a universal researcher policy.

**Falsifier:** any entry that says or operationally implies “CNA scope authorizes testing,” “CVE assignment rights establish bug-bounty/in-scope status,” “CRA voluntary notification authorizes testing,” or “CRA reporting removes liability for the underlying research” violates the boundary. A local synthetic package/worksheet check is allowed; target instructions, target-selection criteria, or a direction to contact/submit to a live target are not. Current reviewed CNA/CRA entries do not meet this falsifier.

## Smallest safety invariant

Every summary of these materials must state the actor and the action: CNA rules govern CVE assignment within CNA scope; CRA Article 14 imposes specified reporting duties on manufacturers, while Article 15 concerns notification only. Neither supplies researcher testing authorization, target scope, safe harbor, or general immunity. Preserve the Q6 documentation-only boundary and refer any actual authorization question to the target owner’s current, explicit program policy; do not infer permission from law, CNA status, a report route, or a notification-liability clause.
