## Evidence and overlap

- **F26's repeated/general rules:** `corpus/knowledge/google-cloud-vrp-customer-resource-boundary.md:9-11` attributes scope to this program and distinguishes scope from permission; this restates F03's safe-harbor/scope separation (`corpus/knowledge/authorization-safe-harbor-and-scope.md:7-9`). The Cloud-only scope and customer-resource prohibition at F26 `:11,16,22` are program-specific instances of F09's rule that platform text cannot supply a third party's consent (`corpus/knowledge/statutory-authorization-and-unauthorized-access.md:41-46`) and F13's distinction between controlled and third-party assets (`corpus/knowledge/program-severity-ladders-and-reward-scales.md:16`). F09's ROE entry likewise says a finding outside the written engagement boundary is unauthorized (`corpus/knowledge/authorised-testing-rules-of-engagement.md:9-20`).
- **Reward/technical separation:** F26 `:17,22` says customer-resource testing can make a report reward-ineligible without erasing the finding or implying permission. This is the Cloud VRP's concrete condition, but the separation principle is already explicit in F01 (`corpus/knowledge/cvss-v4-severity-and-vector.md:34,41-43`). F26 `:15`'s Q5 product-scope statement and `:11`'s customer-domain examples are additional S069-specific facts, not the two rules named as its novel ground.
- **Prior customer-scope overlap omitted from F26's comparison:** F16 already says CNVD intake is limited to customer-authorized project scope and systems outside it are unauthorized (`corpus/knowledge/chinese-crowdsourced-testing-scope-and-rewards.md:21-40`); its 360SRC section also describes third-party-vendor/public-cloud assets as a collection exclusion (`:111-118`). This overlaps the *general* customer/third-party boundary, not either precise S069 rule below. F26's cited F03/F09/F13/F01 examples are accurate but not an exhaustive prior-art list.
- **New discriminator — customer IP allocation:** F26 `:28-32` states that a Google-owned asset within a customer IP block remains unauthorized during broad scanning. S069 capture `corpus/source-snapshots/round-44/S069.txt:73-77` specifically prohibits scanning customer-allocated CIDR/IP ranges and says this remains unauthorized even when the discovered asset is Google-owned. The prior F13 rule at `program-severity-ladders-and-reward-scales.md:16` turns on control/ownership of the asset; F09 `statutory-authorization-and-unauthorized-access.md:43-46` turns on the affected computer's operator/consent. Neither addresses a Google-owned asset inside a customer-allocated address block. The range-level discriminator therefore adds a case that those general rules do not resolve.
- **New method boundary — unsafe multi-tenant reproduction:** F26 `:18,28-30` directs against live testing of unsafe shared-tenant escapes and toward technical analysis/local reproduction. S069 `:78-87` says to use isolated/local environments where possible and not test a risk-to-other-tenants case against the live multi-tenant environment. No such live-vs-local testing rule appears in the examined F01/F03/F09/F13 knowledge files. F09's “hosting tenant” at `statutory-authorization-and-unauthorized-access.md:46` concerns who can consent, not safe reproduction. `doj-vdp-framework-scope-and-authorization.md:44,48` mentions co-resident tenants and third-party interests/contracts, not this testing instruction. F16's project-scope rule is also distinct.
