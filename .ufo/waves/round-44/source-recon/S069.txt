{"title":"Cloud Vulnerability Reward Program Rules", "body":"\nThe Google Cloud Vulnerability Reward Program recognizes the contributions of\nsecurity researchers who invest their time and effort in helping us secure our\nplatform and our customers. Through this program, we provide monetary rewards\nand public recognition for novel vulnerabilities disclosed to us.\n\n## Services in scope\n\nIn principle, any [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/products?e=48754805)\nproduct or web service that handles reasonably sensitive user data is intended\nto be in scope.\n\nOn the flip side, the program has several important exclusions to keep in mind:\n\n* **Google Workspace products** – Products belonging to\n [Google Workspace](https://workspace.google.com/) are out of scope for the\n Cloud VRP and should be reported to the\n [Google VRP](/about/rules/google-friends/6625378258649088/google-and-alphabet-vulnerability-reward-program-vrp-rules)\n instead.\n* **Third-party websites** – Some Google-branded services hosted in less\n common domains may be operated by our vendors or partners. We can't\n authorize you to test these systems on behalf of their owners and will not\n reward such reports. Please read the fine print on the page and examine\n domain and IP WHOIS records to confirm. If in doubt, talk to us first!\n* **Recent acquisitions** – To allow time for internal review and remediation,\n newly acquired companies are subject to a six-month blackout period. Bugs\n reported sooner than that will typically not qualify for a reward.\n\n## Qualifying vulnerabilities\n\nAny design or implementation issue that substantially affects the\nconfidentiality or integrity of user data is likely to be in scope for the\nprogram. Common examples include:\n\n* Cross-site scripting,\n* Cross-site request forgery,\n* Mixed-content scripts,\n* Authentication or authorization flaws,\n* Server-side code execution bugs,\n* [XSLeak bugs](/learn/invalid-reports/web-platform/xsleaks/5022006283862016/xsleaks-and-xs-search).\n\nNote that the scope of the program is limited to technical vulnerabilities in\nGoogle Cloud products and web services; please do not try to sneak into Google\noffices, attempt phishing attacks against our employees, and so on.\n\nOut of concern for the availability of our services to all users, please do not\nattempt to carry out DoS attacks, leverage black hat SEO techniques, spam\npeople, or do other similarly questionable things. We also discourage the use of\nany vulnerability testing tools that automatically generate very significant\nvolumes of traffic.\n\n## Rules of Engagement: Disqualifying Actions & Boundaries\n\nEngaging in prohibited testing or crossing the boundaries described below will\ndisqualify a report from receiving a reward, regardless of the severity or\nnovelty of the vulnerability. Severe or intentional violations may also result\nin suspension from all Google Bug Hunters programs.\n\n### Do Not Test Customer Projects & Resources\n\nThe Google Cloud VRP scope is strictly limited to Google-owned infrastructure\nand services. To protect customer data and privacy, testing customer resources\nis strictly prohibited:\n\n* **No Unauthorized Scanning or Access**: Testing, probing, scanning, or\n interacting with customer instances, databases, storage buckets, or\n applications without written authorization from the customer is prohibited.\n* **Customer Domains**: Customer-owned domains include, but are not limited\n to, `*.bc.googleusercontent.com` and `*.appspot.com`.\n* **Ineligibility**: Even if you find a valid vulnerability in Google-managed\n infrastructure, the report is ineligible for a reward if finding or\n verifying it involved testing or accessing customer resources.\n* **Customer IP Ranges**: Scanning CIDR blocks or IP ranges allocated to\n Google Cloud customers (see\n [Google Cloud IP Ranges](https://www.gstatic.com/ipranges/cloud.json)) is\n prohibited. Finding a Google-owned asset inside a customer IP block during a\n broad scan is still considered unauthorized testing within customer space.\n* **Multi-Tenant Sandbox Escapes & Unsafe Testing**: Where possible, test\n sandbox or isolation escapes in isolated or local environments (such as\n [Compute Engine sole-tenant nodes](https://cloud.google.com/compute/docs/nodes/sole-tenant-nodes),\n a root shell on your own GKE node, or local open-source runtimes like\n upstream gVisor) rather than shared multi-tenant services (such as Cloud\n Run). If you believe you have found a vulnerability that cannot be safely\n tested without risking access or impact to other customers or tenants, **do\n not test it against the live multi-tenant environment**. Instead, submit a\n report with your technical analysis and local reproduction so the Cloud VRP\n team can evaluate it safely.\n\n### Hard Stop at the Google Production Environment (Borg)\n\nAs a researcher, you are not permitted to test Google's production environment,\nBorg. If you discover a vulnerability that grants access to Borg, you must\nadhere to the following principles:\n\n* **Immediate Stop**: As soon as a payload, sandbox escape, SSRF, or RPC gives\n you code execution or access to Borg or Google's internal production\n network, stop all testing immediately.\n* **No Lateral Movement**: Do not attempt lateral movement, internal network\n discovery, credential harvesting, or access to internal services or\n databases (Spanner, Chubby, etc.).\n* **Safe Harbor & Maximum Impact Evaluation**: We evaluate reports based on\n the **maximum potential impact** of the initial compromise. Stopping at the\n boundary keeps you covered by Safe Harbor and eligible for the full reward.\n\n### Researcher Responsibility for Automated Agents & Tools\n\nYou are responsible for all actions and network traffic generated by any\nautomated scanners, fuzzers, custom scripts, or AI agents you run. Configure\nyour tools with strict target boundaries and rate limits. Any rogue or\nunintended actions performed by your agents or tools that impact other tenants\nor Google infrastructure may disqualify your report from a reward, and repeated\nor severe violations may result in removal from all Google Bug Hunters programs.\n\n## Non-qualifying vulnerabilities\n\n### Vulnerabilities within Third-Party Software Components\n\nReports on vulnerabilities where the root cause is within the code of a\nthird-party product that Google Cloud offers as a managed service (e.g., a flaw\nin the core Microsoft SQL Server engine itself, discovered while using Cloud\nSQL) are handled as follows:\n\n* **High Severity Impact (S0):** If the vulnerability is assessed by Google as\n S0 severity **and** it demonstrably impacts the security of Google Cloud\n customers, it may qualify for a reward. Eligible reports will be rewarded\n based on the assessed impact and severity according to the\n standard\n [Reward amounts](/about/rules/google-friends/cloud-vulnerability-reward-program-rules#reward-amounts)\n table, commensurate with the impact on the Google Cloud service.\n* **Lower Severity Impact (S1 or lower):** Vulnerabilities within the\n third-party product code assessed as S1 severity or lower are not eligible\n for rewards from the Google Cloud VRP.\n* **Reporting to the Vendor:** Regardless of the severity, we encourage\n researchers to also disclose the vulnerability directly to the third-party\n vendor. Google is committed to\n [responsible disclosure](https://about.google/company-info/appsecurity/) and\n will notify the vendor of such vulnerabilities reported to us.\n* **Vulnerabilities in Google's implementation**, integration, configuration,\n or management layer *around* the third-party service remain in scope as per\n the standard Cloud VRP rules.\n\n### Other non-qualifying findings\n\n<div class=\"maia-promo\">\n <strong>Note:</strong> Visit our <a href=\n \"/learn\" target=\"_blank\">Bug Hunter\n University</a> page dedicated to common non-qualifying findings and vulnerabilities.\n</div>\n\n<p></p>\n\nThe main reason why a reported issue may not qualify for a reward is because of\nlow impact. Although we review each report on a case-by-case basis, we are\nsharing some of the common low-risk issues that typically do not earn a monetary\nreward:\n\n* **Cross-site scripting vulnerabilities in “sandbox” domains**\n ([*read more*](/learn/invalid-reports/web-platform/xss/6619189462433792).)\n We maintain a number of domains that leverage the same-origin policy to\n safely isolate certain types of untrusted content; the most prominent\n example of this is \"*.googleusercontent.com\". Unless an impact on sensitive\n user data can be demonstrated, we do not consider the ability to execute\n JavaScript in that domain to be a bug.\n* **URL redirection**\n ([*read more*](/learn/invalid-reports/navigation/6680364896223232).) We\n recognize that the address bar is the only reliable security indicator in\n modern browsers; consequently, we hold that the usability and security\n benefits of a small number of well-designed and closely monitored\n redirectors outweigh their true risks.\n* **Legitimate content proxying and framing.** We expect our services to\n unambiguously label third-party content and to perform a number of\n abuse-detection checks, but as with redirectors, we think that the value the\n risk.\n* **Bugs requiring exceedingly unlikely user interaction.** For example, a\n cross-site scripting flaw that requires the victim to manually type in an\n XSS payload and then double-click an error message may realistically not\n meet the bar.\n* **Logout cross-site request forgery**\n ([*read more*](/learn/invalid-reports/web-platform/csrf-clickjacking/5072689380982784).)\n For better or worse, the design of HTTP cookies means that no single website\n can prevent its users from being logged out; consequently,\n application-specific ways of achieving this goal will likely not qualify.\n You may be interested in personal blog posts from\n [Chris Evans](http://scarybeastsecurity.blogspot.com/2010/01/logout-xsrf-significant-web-app-bug.html)\n and\n [Michal Zalewski](http://lcamtuf.blogspot.com/2010/10/http-cookies-or-how-not-to-design.html)\n for more background.\n* **Flaws affecting the users of out-of-date browsers and plugins.** The\n security model of the web is constantly being fine-tuned. The panel\n typically does not reward reports that describe issues that affect only the\n users of outdated or unpatched browsers.\n* **Presence of banner or version information.** Version information does not,\n by itself, expose the service to attacks - so we do not consider this to be\n a bug. That said, if you find outdated software and have good reasons to\n suspect that it poses a well-defined security risk, please let us know.\n* **User enumeration.** Reports outlining user enumeration are not within\n scope unless you can demonstrate that we don't have any rate limits in place\n to protect our users.\n* **Bypassing the limit of accounts that can be verified with a given SMS\n number.** We often receive reports about users being able to bypass our SMS\n limit for verifying accounts. There are actually two different quotas per\n number for account verification, one via 'SMS' and a different one via 'Call\n Me'.\n* **Activity within the scope of your own provisioned resources.** If you have\n root/admin in your own isolated instance (like Cloud Shell, GCE, etc.),\n things you do inside that instance aren't a vulnerability unless you can\n affect other users' or Google's infra. This includes looking at old data on\n your disk.\n* **No PoC**: Reports that lack a functional proof-of-concept.\n* **Customer Misconfiguration / Shared Responsibility**: Insecure customer\n configurations (such as unconditionally injecting shared secrets or\n misconfiguring security-related settings) rather than a product\n vulnerability. This also includes vulnerabilities that exist entirely within\n the application code deployed by the customer.\n* **UI discrepancy**: In some cases the underlying APIs may have a different\n behaviour than the (web) UI on top of it. We usually consider the API to be\n the source of truth. Reports focusing on discrepancies between the UI and\n the API usually don't receive a monetary reward, unless the story around it\n clearly demonstrates the bypass of a security boundary.\n* **Social Engineering Pre-requisite**: Theoretical attacks requiring victims\n to execute double-click scenarios on previously-installed malicious software\n or inserting arbitrary authorized domain strings for Google One Tap prompts\n are generally rejected; these vectors operate externally via social\n engineering rather than indicating platform vulnerabilities.\n\nMonetary rewards aside, vulnerability reporters who work with us to resolve\nsecurity bugs in our products will be credited on the\n[Leaderboard](/leaderboard). If we file an internal security bug, we will\nacknowledge your contribution on that page.\n\n## Reward amounts\n\nThe following table outlines typical rewards for the most common classes of\nbugs.\n\n**Applicable to reports submitted on or after October 1, 2025**\n\n<table bordercolor=\"#ffffff\">\n <tr>\n <th>Severity Impact Category / Impact Tier</th>\n <th>Description</th>\n <th>Google Cloud products on <a href=\"https://github.com/google/bughunters/blob/main/cloud_tiers/cloud-tiers.asciipb\">Tier 1</a> <strong>(IT1)</strong>\n </th>\n <th>Google Cloud products on <a href=\"https://github.com/google/bughunters/blob/main/cloud_tiers/cloud-tiers.asciipb\">Tier 2</a> <strong>(IT2)</strong>\n </th>\n <th>Default Google Cloud products <strong>(IT3)</strong>\n </th>\n <th>Acquired Google Cloud products <strong>(IT3a)</strong>\n </th>\n <th>Other acquisitions or lower priority Google Cloud products <strong>(IT3b, Generic)</strong>\n </th>\n </tr>\n <tr>\n <td colspan=\"7\"><strong>Vulnerabilities without any interaction or relationship between attacker and victim, and the impact must involve unauthorized access to sensitive data or actions (e.g., metadata access may not qualify).</strong>\n </td>\n </tr>\n <tr>\n <td>Root-in-Cloud</td>\n <td>Full administrative takeover (<code>roles/owner</code> equivalence) of <strong>arbitrary Google Cloud organizations or projects without preconditions</strong>, or Remote Code Execution (RCE) in underlying Google production infrastructure (Borg) achieving \"Root-in-Prod\" control (<a href=\"/about/rules/google-friends/cloud-vulnerability-reward-program-rules#root-in-cloud-technical-validation-proof-of-concept\">details</a>).</td>\n <td colspan=\"5\">$250,000</td>\n </tr>\n <tr>\n <td>(S0a) Compromise of Google Cloud Production Environment</td>\n <td>Vulnerabilities that allow an attacker to breach the security boundaries of the underlying Google Infrastructure that runs Cloud services.</td>\n <td colspan=\"3\">$50,000 - Do not probe internal\n production networks, read peer task memory, or access production datastores\n (such as Spanner or Chubby).\n* **Maximum Impact Assessment**: You do not need to show multi-stage lateral\n movement or customer tenant access to qualify for the top reward. The VRP\n panel will evaluate the **direct permissions, credentials, and intended\n service reachability of the compromised Borg job itself** to determine\n whether \"Root-in-Cloud\" control is possible, without chaining through\n separate or already-known internal vulnerabilities in other internal\n systems. Compromises of Borg jobs whose own privileges do not reach\n \"Root-in-Cloud\" impact are rewarded under **(S0a) Compromise of Google Cloud\n Production Environment**.\n\n#### Validation Criteria\n\n1. **Zero Preconditions**: The exploit cannot depend on pre-existing\n permissions in the target project, the existence of a specific product or\n service in the target project, non-default customer configurations, or\n victim interaction.\n1. **Responsible Stopping**: For Path B, you must follow the Borg Boundary Hard\n Stop to qualify for maximum impact evaluation.\n1. **Direct Impact (No Unrelated Internal Chaining)**: For Path B,\n \"Root-in-Cloud\" eligibility is based on the privileges and reachability\n inherent to the compromised production job itself, not on hypothetical\n lateral movement that relies on separate, unrelated, or previously known\n internal vulnerabilities.\n\n### Tiering\n\n#### Understanding impact tiers in Cloud VRP rewards\n\nGoogle Cloud VRP rewards are influenced by the \"Impact Tier\" (IT) of the\naffected product, alongside vulnerability severity.\n\n* **What are Impact Tiers?** We group Google Cloud products into tiers (e.g.,\n IT1, IT2, IT3) based on a combination of multiple factors including the\n number of impacted users, operational dependencies, data sensitivity, etc.\n IT1 includes products with the highest potential impact.\n* **Why Use Tiers?** This system helps us:\n * Align rewards with the potential impact of a vulnerability.\n * Encourage research on our most critical services.\n * Maintain consistency in reward amounts.\n* **Perceived vs. Actual Tier:** A product's tier is based on a\n Google-internal risk assessment across our entire portfolio. While you might\n view a product as highly critical, its tier reflects its relative standing\n within our VRP reward structure. That being said, we do sometimes update our\n tiering, you can monitor those changes using the change history in our\n [Cloud VRP Product Tier List](https://github.com/google/bughunters/blob/main/cloud_tiers/cloud-tiers.asciipb).\n\nIn summary, this tiered approach helps us run a fair and impact-based VRP.\n\n#### How we determine reward tier on Cloud VRP\n\nGoogle Cloud services often integrate components from other products. Usually,\nthe reward tier for a vulnerability is based on the product or component where\nthe flaw actually originates, not necessarily the tier of the service through\nwhich it was discovered.\n\nIf a vulnerability is found within a Tier 1 (IT1) product, but the root cause is\nin an integrated component from a Tier 3 (IT3) product, the vulnerability will\nlikely be assessed against the IT3 tier for reward purposes.
