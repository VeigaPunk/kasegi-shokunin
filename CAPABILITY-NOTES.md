# Practical capability notes: vulnerability evidence and coordination

These are publication summaries of reviewed learning, not new training rounds, new standards or claims of operational experience. The preserved reports and independent judgments govern scope.

## Round64: coordinating multiple affected parties

Track each party's verified product exposure, communication owner, decision authority and evidence. Acknowledging a message is not agreeing to a schedule. A released upstream repair does not prove downstream integration or deployment. Separate available, validated, distributed and installed states. When credible exploitation or an unexpected public disclosure appears, revisit the coordination plan and explain which premise changed. Information already public does not automatically authorize releasing additional enabling detail. A preliminary defensive communication can be useful while the technical record remains incomplete.

Ask for decision-changing evidence and a named owner rather than merely requesting more information. Keep facts, proposed actions and agreements distinct. The actual exercise used four sequential fictional states with independent manual review; it did not contact vendors or prove general real-incident performance.

## Round65: portfolio choices without laundering probability

A probability, a percentile rank and local compromise risk are different quantities. A vulnerability appearing on several inventory rows is not several independent vulnerability events. For distinct indicators, expected count is the sum of probabilities. The probability of at least one event additionally depends on their relationship: independence is a model assumption, not a default fact. A union bound remains useful when that relationship is unknown.

Preserve direct exploitation evidence even when a model score is small. Make capacity constraints and indivisible work costs explicit. Recompute a recommendation when the budget, evidence window, applicability or coupling changes, without mixing values from different windows. Explain residual uncertainty and what would alter the choice. The reviewed three-stage synthetic example supports bounded judgment, not an empirically validated production prioritizer.

## Round66: handoffs across package, product and deployment boundaries

Bind each conclusion to its actual subject: package/version, product/build/configuration, deployed instance, evidence date and responsible owner. A package's affectedness record does not itself prove a product is exploitable. Product-specific code absence does not erase the upstream record. A tested candidate does not establish installation everywhere. Trace which downstream decisions depend on a changed fact; preserve unaffected conclusions and reopen only those whose premises changed.

For a remedy, state cohort-specific validation, rollout proof, residual exposure, owner and next review trigger. Do not close every product line because one build passed. Preserve conflicting claims until evidence resolves their shared subject, rather than selecting the newest timestamp by habit.

Important rejection: the original round66 capability claim was not admitted because teaching preceded the formal predicate freeze. Later successful fictional decisions did not repair that chronology. Keep this distinction when reusing the useful practice. No new credit is earned by this export.
